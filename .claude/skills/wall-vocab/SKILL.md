# Wall Vocab — Brand Language & Voice Skill

## Who Wall Vocab Is

Wall Vocab is the **D2C wall art brand of Space Crafter Studio** (Kingar Signs Pvt Ltd, Bangalore).
Where Space Crafter builds branded environments for companies, Wall Vocab makes **words you put on your wall** — designed and fabricated in the same studio, sold directly to people.

**Product families:**

- Neon quote signs (LED neon: quotes, fandom phrases, symbols)
- 3D letters and moss letter pieces
- Typographic wall art and statement pieces
- Pop-culture and fandom wall pieces (layered acrylic, MDF)
- Interactive wall objects (wall games, custom name pieces)

**Buyers:** people furnishing homes, home offices, creator setups, studios, cafés, and small businesses in India. Price range roughly ₹1,500–₹25,000. Currency is INR. Many pieces are customizable — that is a core selling point, because the studio behind it is a real fabrication house, not a dropshipper.

**The one-line brand idea:** *Your wall has a vocabulary. Choose it well.*

## The Wall Vocab Voice

**Premium minimal, worn casually.** Like a well-made object: nothing extra, everything intentional.

- **Short lines that land.** The product is words on walls. The copy should read like it belongs on one.
- **Warm, not bubbly.** Speak to one person. "Your wall", "your desk", "the room you work in."
- **Object-first.** Name the material, the glow, the finish, the size. Concrete detail is the luxury signal.
- **Quietly confident.** No pleading, no hype. The piece doesn't beg for attention; neither does the copy.
- **Made-by-a-real-studio.** 30+ years of fabrication behind every piece. Mention craft naturally, never as a boast.
- **Feeling over feature — but earn it.** Say what the piece does to a room, then back it with one concrete fact.

### Hard Rules (What to Kill on Sight)

The existing listings were written in emoji-hype voice. That voice is dead. Never use:

- Emoji strings or emoji openers (🌈💖🐐). One emoji max in social captions, zero in product copy.
- Exclamation stacking. One exclamation mark per piece of copy, ideally zero.
- *Must-have, elevate your space, brighten up your space, stunning, aesthetic vibes, perfect addition to, calling all…, love is in the air, take it to the next level, dreamy*
- ALL-CAPS hype words, "Buy now!!" urgency, fake scarcity
- Overexplaining the obvious ("this sign will light up when you turn it on")
- Generic flattery of the buyer ("for champions and high achievers")
- Em dashes in social copy; restructure or use full stops instead

**Rewrite test:** if a line could appear on any Amazon listing, it is not Wall Vocab. Rewrite until it could only be ours.

## The Three Surfaces

### 1. Social Media (Instagram-first)

Instagram is the shop window. Every post sells one of three things: a piece, a feeling, or the craft.

- **Captions:** 1–4 short lines. First line does the work. 3–5 hashtags, niche over broad.
- **Carousels:** Slide 1 is a statement worthy of a wall. Slides 2–6 show the piece, the detail, the room. Last slide: quiet CTA.
- **Reels:** Show the glow, the unboxing, the make. On-screen text under 6 words per beat. Never narrate what's visible.
- **Ads:** One hook line + one concrete product fact + one clear CTA. Premium tone survives even in performance copy.

### 2. Brand Artifacts (Product Pages, Packaging, Email)

**Product page structure — use this exact template:**

```
Title: [Piece name] — [material/type, plainly stated]
One-liner: A single line about what it does to a room.
Description: 2 short paragraphs.
  P1 — the feeling and where it lives (bedroom, desk, studio, café wall).
  P2 — the make: material, light quality, size options, customization, who builds it.
Specs: bullet list — size, material, power, mounting, customization, lead time.
```

**Packaging insert / thank-you card:** 2–3 lines max. Warm, personal, signed off as the studio. It should feel like a note, not marketing.

**Email:** subject lines under 6 words, body under 120 words, one CTA.

### 3. Retail Spatial Copy (Windows, In-Store, Experience Walls)

Wall Vocab retail spaces will eventually exist. Copy written for physical space follows wall logic:

- **Window copy:** under 7 words. Readable in 2 seconds from across a street. It should make someone stop, not inform them.
- **In-store statements:** lines that could themselves be products. Write copy people photograph.
- **Wayfinding/section labels:** one or two words, wit allowed, confusion not.
- Coordinate with the `space-crafter-studio` skill for fabrication/spatial thinking; keep the *language* in Wall Vocab voice.

## How to Use This Skill

1. **Identify the surface** — social, brand artifact, or retail spatial.
2. **Identify what's being sold** — a specific piece, a feeling, or the craft story.
3. **Write object-first**: get one concrete detail (material, glow, size, make) into the copy. If none was provided, ask for the piece or pull it from the Shopify listing.
4. **Apply the format template** for that surface.
5. **Run the hard-rules check** — kill hype, emojis, banned phrases; run the rewrite test.
6. **Keep INR, Indian context, and studio heritage** in frame where relevant.

When in doubt: fewer words, better ones. That's the brand.
