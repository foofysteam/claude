# Pitch library

Five variants. Each solves a different problem for a different reader. Do not mix them.

## Variant A — Sign companies and architectural contractors

**Reader:** owns the client, does survey and install, runs their own floor.
**Their problem:** capacity, not capability. They can make anything. The question is what it costs in a week when three rollouts land together.
**Subject:** `Fabrication capacity from India for [Company]`

```
Hi [Company] team,

[PERSONALISED OPENER — 1-2 sentences naming something specific and true]

I run Space Crafter Studio in Bangalore. We design and manufacture signage and
spatial branding: built-up and flat cut letters, reverse lit and edge lit,
fabricated aluminium and stainless, acrylic, lightboxes, facade identity and
full wayfinding suites. [CREDENTIAL PARAGRAPH]

What I am proposing is simple. You keep the client, the survey and the install.
We take design development, shop drawings and fabrication, and ship finished
goods crated and ready to fix. Either as overflow when the floor is full, or as
a standing line on the products where our cost base helps you most.

[OPTIONAL TIMING PARAGRAPH — see note below]

If it is worth exploring, 15 to 20 minutes on a call. I will show you what we
actually make, how we handle drawings and QC, and what freight looks like door
to door. If the numbers do not work for you, you will know inside that call.
```

**On the FX paragraph.** The rupee-at-record-lows line was used in the Australia and UK campaigns as a reason to write *now*. Use it sparingly and never as the lead. It tells a buyer your advantage is currency rather than craft, and anyone who replies on price alone leaves when the rate moves. The replies worth having ask about drawings, tolerance and lead time.

## Variant B — Trade-only manufacturers

**Reader:** sells fabrication *to* other sign companies. Refuses end users.
**Why A fails here:** "you keep the client and the install" is meaningless. Their client *is* a sign company.
**Their problem:** their own floor is the ceiling on their business.
**Subject:** `Component supply from India for [Company]`

```
Hi [Company] team,

[PERSONALISED OPENER]

I run Space Crafter Studio in Bangalore. We manufacture signage: built-up and
flat cut letters, reverse lit and edge lit, fabricated aluminium and stainless,
acrylic, lightboxes and full wayfinding suites. [CREDENTIAL PARAGRAPH]

You already sell fabrication as a product, so I will be direct about what this
is. Component supply into your production, made to your spec and your
tolerances, shipped crated. You stay the [UK/Australian/local] facing brand and
you keep the trade relationship. We would sit upstream of you, not beside you.

[OPTIONAL TIMING PARAGRAPH]

If it is worth exploring, 15 to 20 minutes on a call. I will show you what we
make, how we hold tolerance, and what freight and lead time look like door to
door. Happy to run a sample part before any of that, if you would rather judge
the work than the conversation.
```

**"Upstream of you, not beside you"** is the line that does the work. It answers the unspoken objection: *are you trying to become my competitor?*

**Expect fewer yeses here.** You are asking a manufacturer to put someone upstream of their own manufacturing, which touches margin, QC and identity. Each yes is worth far more than a sign company's.

## Variant C — Brand identity studios

**Reader:** builds brand systems, hands off physical execution.
**Their problem:** loss of control at the last mile. Not cost.
**Subject:** `The last mile of your brand work`

```
Hi [Studio] team,

[PERSONALISED OPENER]

Here is the pattern I keep seeing. A studio spends three months getting a brand
right. Type, colour, tone, the whole system, documented properly. Then it goes
to whichever signage vendor the client's contractor suggested. The letterforms
get fattened to save on acrylic, the white comes out wrong under LED, and the
wall sits 40mm off centre. The client sees the wall, not the brand book. And it
is your name on the work.

I run Space Crafter Studio in Bangalore. We design and manufacture the physical
side of brand identity: reception walls, 3D and lit lettering, facade identity,
wayfinding systems, environmental graphics and experience installations.
[CREDENTIAL PARAGRAPH]

What I am proposing is a standing arrangement rather than a job. You build the
brand and you own the client. We build the artifacts that make it physical, to
your specification and under your direction, white-labelled if that is how you
would rather present it. You add a margin, or take a referral, whichever fits
how you work.

The reason it is worth ten minutes: you are almost certainly already fielding
these requests and handing them somewhere. That is revenue leaving the studio,
and it is also the part of the project you have the least control over.

15 to 20 minutes on a call. I will show you what we make, how we read a brand
book, and how the commercial side would work.
```

**The 40mm detail matters.** Specific, physical and instantly recognisable to anyone who has watched their work get built badly. Generic versions of this paragraph do not land.

## Variant D — Agencies already selling spatial work

**Reader:** advertises environmental, spatial or experience design with no fabrication behind it.
**Their problem:** they sell something they cannot make.
**Subject:** `The shop behind your environmental branding`

```
Hi [Studio] team,

[PERSONALISED OPENER — must reference the specific service line on their site]

Which means you are already buying the fabrication in somewhere, or turning the
work away because the execution risk is not worth the fee.

I run Space Crafter Studio in Bangalore. We manufacture the physical side of
brand identity: reception walls, 3D and lit lettering, facade identity,
wayfinding systems, environmental graphics and experience installations.
[CREDENTIAL PARAGRAPH]

What I am proposing is that we become the shop behind that line on your services
page. You keep the client, the concept and the direction. We take the shop
drawings, the material decisions, the fabrication and the install. White-
labelled, so it stays your work throughout.

Most studios lose control at exactly this point. The brand book specifies one
white and the vendor delivers another. The letterform gets fattened to save
acrylic. The wall is what the client remembers. We are the version of that
supplier who reads the brand book before quoting.

15 to 20 minutes on a call. I will show you what we make and how the commercial
side would work.
```

**Highest-intent variant in the library.** These agencies have already decided the service is worth selling. You are not creating demand, only replacing a supplier.

## Variant E — End clients (trigger-based)

**Reader:** a facilities, projects or expansion lead with a building opening.
**Their problem:** the opening date, and whether the place will look like the brand.
**Subject:** name the project. `Signage for [the specific project]`

```
[Name],

[THE TRIGGER — one or two sentences proving you noticed something specific and
current about their project. This is the whole email. Get it right or do not
send it.]

[WHAT THAT KIND OF PROJECT ACTUALLY NEEDS — two or three items, not a service
list. For a hospital: wayfinding, departmental signage, exterior identity. For a
store: storefront, interior graphics, window. Show you understand the build,
not just the category.]

We are Space Crafter Studio in Bangalore. We design and manufacture signage and
spatial branding, and we came out of Aarkay Signs which started in 1989.
[ONE relevant proof point — a comparable project type, not a client list dump.]

If the signage is not locked yet, I would like 15 to 20 minutes to understand
the programme and show you what we would propose. If it is already awarded, tell
me and I will leave it there.
```

### Rules for Variant E

- **Lead with their project. Never with your company.**
- **One proof point, not a client list.** For a hospital, cite hospital work. Nothing else is relevant to them.
- **Give an explicit exit** — "if it is already awarded, tell me and I will leave it there." Costs nothing, and it reads as someone who respects their time rather than someone working a list.
- **Never guess the budget or the timeline.** Being wrong in the first email ends it.
- **Verify the address.** All four bounces in July 2026 were end-client emails to constructed addresses. If the individual's address cannot be verified, use the published corporate or projects inbox and ask for them by name in the body.

## Writing the personalised opener

The opener is the only part a reader uses to decide whether the rest is worth reading. It must be **specific, true, and impossible to send to anyone else.**

**Good openers name a fact and draw a consequence:**

> "You already run a facility in Portugal, so offshore manufacturing is not a new idea to you and I will not waste the email explaining the concept."

> "Environment branding is already a line on your services page, and Michelin, Hyatt and The Hindu are clients where that line gets used."

> "Two thousand stores for Starbucks, Tanishq, Blue Tokai and Shoppers Stop. That is a great deal of in-store graphics leaving your studio as a drawing and arriving as somebody else's fabrication."

> "Working out of Darwin means everything you do not make yourself already arrives after a long freight leg. That changes the maths on offshore supply in your favour rather than against it."

**Bad openers could be sent to anyone:**

> "I came across your website and was impressed by your work."
> "As a leading signage company, you must face challenges with capacity."

### Two techniques that lift reply rates

**Concede something.** A cold email that admits a limit gets the rest of it believed.

> "Your plastics capability is deeper than ours and I am not going to pretend otherwise."
> "Chemically etched metal is a specialist chemistry we do not run and will not pretend to."
> "The hand-carved work is not something anyone should outsource and I am not suggesting it."

**Disqualify openly when the fit is weak.** Better than a vague pitch, and occasionally it is the thing that gets a reply.

> "Shop signs and vehicle livery is quick-turn work where sea freight from India is no use to you at all. I am writing only because most sign firms also carry longer-lead fabricated jobs. If you do not, say so and I will leave it there."
