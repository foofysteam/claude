# Export markets and trade agreements

Verified August 2026. Re-verify before quoting a duty rate to anyone.

---

## Read this before anything else

**For fabricated signage, duty is almost never the binding constraint.** Three things decide whether an export market works, and none of them is a trade agreement:

1. **Freight cube.** Built-up letters and lightboxes are bulky and low value-density. A 5% duty saving disappears inside one badly packed LCL consignment. The exportable products are the ones that flat-pack: acrylic faces, aluminium returns and trims, LED modules, wayfinding panels, components for local assembly. Fully assembled 3D pylons are rarely economic to ship.
2. **Installation is local and licensed.** UAE requires municipality-approved signage contractors. UK and Australia require working-at-height, CDM and electrical competencies. The realistic model is **supply-and-support with a local install partner**, not turnkey export.
3. **Certification for anything mains-powered.** UKCA, CE, RCM/EESS (Australia), PSE (Japan), ECAS/Emirates Quality Mark (UAE). A real per-product-family cost, and a bigger barrier than any tariff below.

If Akshay or anyone else frames a market choice purely on trade agreements, say this.

**The highest-probability entry route is following existing clients.** Indian brands, hospitality groups and QSR chains expanding into the GCC or UK will accept an Indian fabricator they already trust. That bypasses the cold-start problem entirely.

## HS codes — use the current ones

**HS 9405.60 no longer exists.** HS 2022 split it. Using the dead code is a common reason preference claims get rejected.

| Product | Code |
|---|---|
| Illuminated signs, lightboxes, LED channel letters | **9405.61** (solely LED) |
| Non-LED illuminated signs | 9405.69 |
| Non-illuminated built-up metal letters, name-plates, plaques | **8310.00** |
| Fabricated aluminium frames, structures, pylon substructure | **7610.90** |
| Acrylic panels and formed components | **3926.90** |
| Fabricated stainless articles not in 8310 | 7326.90 |

## In force — real duty position today

### UAE — India–UAE CEPA
- In force **1 May 2022**
- UAE standard MFN is **5% CIF**. CEPA eliminates duty on 97%+ of tariff lines covering 99% of Indian exports by value; 80.3% duty-free immediately. Signage sits in the immediate group. **Effective 5% → 0%.**
- **Rules of origin are strict:** qualifying value content **at least 40% of FOB** *and* change in tariff classification at 4- or 6-digit level. Both, not either.
- Watch transshipment scrutiny — the UAE has been under pressure on CEPA transshipment claims.
- Sources: [UAE Ministry of Economy](https://www.moet.gov.ae/en/cepa_india), [Govt of India CEPA FAQ](https://www.nsez.gov.in/Resources/Trade/FAQs%20on%20CEPA.pdf)

### United Kingdom — India–UK CETA
- Signed 24 July 2025, **in force 15 July 2026**. Very recent.
- **Zero duty on ~99% of India's exports.** Engineering goods previously facing up to 18% go to zero.
- Rules of origin from the official UK text:
  - `3926` — CTSH, or chemical reaction (easiest)
  - `8310` — CTSH **and** Standard QVC
  - `9405` — **CTH** and Standard QVC. Note CTH not CTSH: you cannot make an illuminated sign from imported parts already classified in 9405.
  - Chapter 76 — CTSH and Standard QVC
- Standard QVC reported as 35% ex-works build-up, or 40% ex-works / 45% FOB build-down, de minimis 12.5%. **These percentages come from a professional-services summary, not the primary text — confirm before building a costing model.**
- CETA uses co-equal rules: where a product has more than one rule, you may choose whichever you satisfy.
- Sources: [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2274280&reg=48&lang=2), [UK PSR Annex 3A](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/687e5e378adf4250705c96db/uk_india_ceta_annex3a_product_specific_rules_of_origin.pdf)

### Australia — India–Australia ECTA
- In force 29 December 2022. Australia made 96% duty-free immediately; staged lines completed **1 January 2026**. Effectively **everything from India now enters at 0%** against a 5% general rate.
- ROO where no PSR applies: CTSH plus QVC 35% build-up or 45% build-down of FOB. CoO valid 12 months, records kept 5 years.
- Broader CECA still under negotiation. ECTA already gives 0%, so CECA is not a precondition.
- Source: [DFAT](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/using-ecta-do-business-india.pdf)

### Oman — India–Oman CEPA
- **In force 1 June 2026.** New, low competition, useful second GCC foothold.
- **Line-level treatment of signage codes not verified.** The PIB release itemises textiles and handicrafts, not engineering goods. Broad coverage is likely but confirm before quoting.

### Markets where the agreement buys nothing

- **Switzerland / EFTA TEPA** — in force 1 Oct 2025, but Switzerland **abolished duty on all industrial goods for all countries on 1 Jan 2024**. Zero preferential margin. Ignore for goods.
- **Japan CEPA** — verified line by line: 9405.61, 9405.69 and 8310 are already **Free at MFN** for everyone. Only 7610.90 shows a margin (3% MFN vs 1.4% EPA). Duty is a non-issue; language, PSE certification and spec culture are the barriers.
- **Singapore CECA** — Singapore levies duty on only four categories: liquor, tobacco, motor vehicles, petroleum. Everything else is 0% MFN. **Do not pay for Certificates of Origin.** Still valuable as a client-acquisition and regional-HQ market.
- **South Korea CEPA** — in force since 2010, upgrade negotiations ongoing (12th round May 2026). Category assignment for signage codes **not verified**; Korea MFN is generally 8% and the gap between an eliminated line and a "RED" line (1–5%) is material. Check before pricing.

### Smaller / partial

- **ASEAN AITIGA** — in force 2010. **The review has NOT concluded** — 13th Joint Committee July 2026 still had open chapters. Do not plan around an upgrade. ROO: 35% FOB content **and** CTSH.
- **Malaysia MICECA** — in force 2011, runs parallel to AITIGA, elect whichever is better. Line-level not verified.
- **Thailand** — **no comprehensive FTA.** Only the 82-item Early Harvest Scheme, which contains **no Chapter 83 items and none of the signage codes**. Access is via AITIGA only.
- **Mauritius CECPA** — in force 2021 but covers a limited list, not blanket liberalisation. **Assume exclusion until confirmed.** Tiny market.
- **Sri Lanka ISFTA** — in force 2000, but Sri Lanka maintains a **1,220-line negative list** weighted toward finished construction goods. Signage may well be excluded. Verify with Sri Lanka Customs. Closest market geographically, highest payment risk. Advance payment or confirmed LC only.

## Ranked shortlist

**Tier 1 — pursue**

1. **UAE** — 5% to 0%, enormous continuous fit-out pipeline, English, dense Indian contractor network, cheapest freight, air freight viable. Best tariff-to-opportunity ratio by a wide margin. Needs a local partner for permits and install. Moderate payment risk: Gulf construction has a late-payment and retention culture, so contract carefully.
2. **United Kingdom** — duty-free since 15 July 2026 and most exporters have not repriced, so there is a first-mover window. Best payment discipline and contract enforcement on the list. Against it: 3–4 week freight, UKCA cost, strong domestic sign industry. The absolute tariff gain is small, so win on capability, not duty.
3. **Saudi Arabia and wider GCC** — no FTA, 5% duty, go anyway. The construction pipeline dwarfs everything else here. The duty is not what stops you; local content rules and the local-partner requirement are.

**Tier 2 — selective**

4. **Australia** — fully duty-free, English, excellent payment security. Held back by small population, long freight, strict RCM/EESS electrical and AS/NZS wind-load compliance. Pursue with a named rollout partner, not speculatively.
5. **Singapore** — zero duty regardless, short freight, near-zero payment risk, regional HQ for brands rolling out across Southeast Asia. Client-acquisition market, not a volume market.
6. **Oman** — new, low competition, likely zero duty though unverified.
7. **Malaysia / ASEAN** — low duty, close, English usable. Competing directly against Chinese and local fabricators with structurally lower costs. Thin margins.

**Tier 3 — watch**

8. **EU** — big prize, in force ~Q2 2027. Nothing actionable until then.
9. **Sri Lanka** — closest, but possible negative-list exclusion and the highest payment risk.
10. **Japan, South Korea** — no meaningful duty advantage, non-English, certification-heavy. Skip unless a client drags you there.
11. **Switzerland / EFTA** — zero preferential margin. Ignore.
12. **Canada, NZ, Israel, Chile, Peru, Mauritius** — not in force, too small, or unverified.

## Two cheap next steps

1. Run the four HS codes through the [UK Trade Tariff tool](https://www.trade-tariff.service.gov.uk) and the UAE Ministry of Economy market access dashboard. Both free, about an hour, and confirms the exact rates.
2. Cost a trial flat-pack consignment to Dubai and to London. Freight, not duty, will tell you whether any of this works.

## Not verified — do not state as fact

- Exact UK and EU MFN rates for 3926.90, 7610.90, 8310.00, 9405.61. Confident they are low single digits (~2–6.5%) but the tariff services block automated retrieval.
- Line-level treatment under Oman CEPA, Mauritius CECPA, Sri Lanka ISFTA, Korea CEPA, Malaysia MICECA, and each ASEAN member's AITIGA schedule. Only Japan was verified line by line.
- Whether India still qualifies for Japan's GSP on 7610.90 — the schedule shows GSP "Free" versus India EPA 1.4%, which would make GSP the better route. Worth ten minutes with a customs broker.
- Signage market-size figures. Every available number is paywalled commercial research that mixes "digital signage" (LED screens, media players) with fabricated signage. Not comparable. Keep market commentary qualitative.
